Seven ways into the same text: when things happened, where they happened, who descended from whom, how the parts quote and echo one another, where each book's world sits, and the words themselves with every reference made walkable.

Old Testament and New, 66 books, built from public-domain text and openly licensed scholarly data. Nothing here smooths over disagreement — where a date or a location is contested, the view says so.

How to read the colours

Attested Fixed by a source outside the Bible — an inscription, a Babylonian chronicle, a datable reign. The fall of Samaria in 722 BCE, Gallio's proconsulship in 51 CE.
Conventional The date most scholars work with, inferred from the text and its setting rather than documented. Solomon's temple, the crucifixion.
Traditional Derived from the Bible's own internal chronology, with no external anchor at all. Everything before the monarchy sits here.
Disputed Serious competing proposals exist. The Exodus appears twice, at 1446 and at 1260 BCE, because the evidence genuinely points both ways.

Where the data comes from

TextKing James Version, public domain.
Cross-referencesOpenBible.info, CC-BY — 344,798 usable links, each carrying the votes readers gave it.
GeographyBible Geocoding Data, CC-BY — every identifiable place, with the scholarly confidence in each siting.
ChronologyHand-authored for this project from standard reference works, and validated against the text on every build. See data/curated/.
GenealogyHand-authored from Genesis 5 and 11, Ruth 4, Matthew 1 and Luke 3. Both New Testament genealogies are recorded in full, unreconciled.
Translationsscrollmapper \u2014 Wycliffe (c.1395), Tyndale (1525\u201330), Darby (1890), Young's Literal (1898) and the ASV (1901), all public domain.
